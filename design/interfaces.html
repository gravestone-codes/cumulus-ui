Interfaces: switch and group

Every runtime value below is sample data. In the app, all of it comes from the backend. A group is a set of switches (for example DC1-leaf, with leaf-01 to leaf-04). Group screens read every member in parallel. Group writes fan out: each switch gets its own branch, its own dry-run diff, and its own result, through the existing FanOut engine.

How to choose: each numbered section is one decision with lettered options. Reply with your picks, for example "1A, 2C, 3B, 4A, 5A, 6B, 7A, 8B, 9A", plus any tweaks. ● marks my recommendation. Section 10 is the full catalogue of what an interface can do, with single-switch and group support and build status.

9Scope: how much "everything interfaces can do" right now

The roadmap (§3A) says interfaces are thin first: Bridge, VRF, routing and QoS slices come later, and each one adds its tab or card to the interface page as a consumer. You asked for everything. Those two pull in different directions, so this one is your call.

AInterface-owned areas now; domain areas land with their slice ● recommended

Build now: link (all leaves), breakout, flap protection, counters and clear, transceiver, PHY, LLDP, neighbors (ARP/ND), IP and VRF membership, bonds (BondBuilder), storm control, port security, 802.1X, sFlow, telemetry, plus group versions of all of these. Bridge/VLAN, VRR/VRRP, EVPN-MH, OSPF/PIM/PBR, QoS and ACL cards ship with slices 3B to 3G, reusing the same card and fan-out pieces. Keeps the roadmap's "shallow before deep" ordering.

BEverything in section 10 now

Every row in the catalogue gets built under Interfaces now, including bridge, VRR/VRRP, OSPF/PIM, QoS and ACL bindings. It's faster to a "complete interface page", but it builds bridge/QoS/ACL forms before their own domain screens exist, which risks reworking them later. It changes roadmap §3A, so I'd update the roadmap with your sign-off.
